## 2. Feature gaps vs. real competitors (sourced research, not generic "best practice")

A dedicated research pass looked at Preply, Superprof, Urban Company, Practo, Sulekha, and UrbanPro — not to find one company TeacherCircle should copy wholesale (none of them run the same "free listing + first-message-then-reveal" model TeacherCircle does), but to pull out the specific, evidenced mechanisms worth adapting:

1. **Verification tiers vary hugely, and the badge should say which tier you're at.** Preply does government-ID + credential upload (blue check) but explicitly *no* criminal background check. Urban Company does third-party background checks (via AuthBridge) plus an ongoing minimum-rating bar to stay listed. Practo cross-checks a real registry number (state medical council / NMC) against an external authority — the strongest, most objectively-checkable model of the five, and the most transferable to teachers if a public teaching-credential registry exists in the markets TeacherCircle serves. TeacherCircle currently has **zero** verification of any kind on a teacher profile beyond "an email address exists." A `photo_url` column has existed on `teacher_profile` since the original schema and has never been wired to anything. — ✅ **Done (P2)**: a self-attestation checkbox, explicitly labeled as *not* a background/identity check — the honest, feasible version of this given no paid verification vendor. `photo_url` remains unwired (out of scope).
2. **Reviews should be gated to a real, verifiable interaction, or they're easy to game.** Preply only allows a review from a student who *paid* for a lesson with that specific tutor. TeacherCircle already does the closest available equivalent — a review requires a prior `contact_request` — which is good and already matches this principle; worth keeping and not weakening.
3. **Response-time and completed-interaction signals are cheap, high-trust additions.** Preply surfaces "replies within 24h"; Urban Company enforces a minimum customer rating to stay listed. TeacherCircle already has the raw data to compute "responds within X hours" (message timestamps) and could surface it on a teacher card with no new schema. — ✅ **Done (P2)**: `teacher_response_time`, bucketed into a low-precision label, hidden below 3 data points.
4. **A visible in-app report + block mechanism is the baseline, not a nice-to-have**, once strangers can message each other — confirmed as standard across the general marketplace trust-and-safety literature (not company-specific, but consistently cited). This is genuinely missing today (see Security finding #5 above) and is the single highest-priority gap this review found. — ✅ **Done (P0)**.
5. **Explicit gap in the research itself, disclosed rather than papered over**: no 2026 source was found that specifically standardizes age-verification or parental-consent flows for a *tutoring* platform (as opposed to general social platforms). Since TeacherCircle's messaging can put a parent/guardian on one side and an adult tutor on the other, this is a genuine open question worth raising with a lawyer, not something any competitor's practice already answers — flagging it rather than guessing at a "standard" that doesn't demonstrably exist. — ⬜ **Still open** — a legal question, not an engineering one; not addressed by P0–P3.
6. **SEO is completely unbuilt** and is realistically TeacherCircle's main growth channel as a free directory (no commission funding paid acquisition). No per-teacher `<meta>` tags, no `sitemap.xml`, no `robots.txt`, no structured data. This was flagged as a suggestion before messaging shipped and remains untouched. — ✅ **Done (P1)**: sitemap, robots.txt, per-teacher metadata + schema.org JSON-LD.

## 4. Prioritized recommendation

**P0 (do next, trust & safety):** report + block in messaging. This is the one gap that's both a real user-safety issue today (messaging shipped without it) and directly evidenced as a baseline expectation across every competitor researched.

**P1:** basic rate limiting on `/api/auth/*`, `/api/reviews`, `/api/conversations/*` — cheap to add (a simple in-memory or Redis-backed limiter; Redis is already provisioned, just unused), closes a real abuse vector.

**P1:** SEO basics (`sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`, per-teacher meta tags, schema.org `Person`/`Service` structured data) — highest-leverage growth lever available, zero ongoing cost.

**P2:** teacher verification, even a lightweight self-attested "ID uploaded, reviewed by admin" tier surfaced honestly as exactly that (not oversold as a background check) — directly modeled on Practo's registry-check pattern, scaled down to what's actually feasible without a paid verification vendor.

**P2:** response-time signal on teacher cards ("usually replies within a few hours") — free, from data already collected.

**P3:** search pagination, security headers (`next.config.js`), server-side password strength check, dark mode, saved teachers, richer filters.
